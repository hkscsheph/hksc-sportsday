    How timing works

    The whole picture is the finish line

    This is the same idea as a fully automatic photo finish. The camera does not freeze one moment. It keeps a thin vertical strip on the finish and places each moment beside the next. Moving left and right moves through time.

    1. Put the phone a few metres to the side, held still, with the white line exactly on the finish. Portrait keeps more of each runner.
    2. Start the clock when the race starts. Hit record as they arrive, or turn on recording with the clock if the phone is on a stand.
    3. On the photo, put the centre line on the front of the torso — not the head, hands, or feet — and mark that time.

    Slice width is how much of the picture is taken from each frame. Match it to the runners so bodies look whole. The clock text is the time at the centre line. A phone camera is usually 30 or 60 frames a second, so that is the real step between pictures.
